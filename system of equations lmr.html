01 · What is a System of Equations?
∑

Definition & StructureTwo or more equations, same variables

General Form
ax + by = c
dx + ey = f
Find values of x and y that satisfy both equations simultaneously
  • ▶A solution is an ordered pair (x, y) that makes both equations true
  • ▶Graphically: the solution is the point of intersection
  • ▶Every system has exactly 0, 1, or infinitely many solutions
  • ▶SAT: ~3–5 system questions per test — know all three solving methods
Example System
2x + y = 7
x − y = 2
→ Solution: (3, 1)
📊

Three Solution Types at a GlanceThe most tested concept on SAT for systems

✓ Exactly One Solution
CONDITION
m₁ ≠ m₂ (different slopes)
Lines cross at exactly one point. The (x,y) coordinates of that point are the solution.
✗ No Solution
CONDITION
m₁ = m₂, b₁ ≠ b₂
Parallel lines — never meet. System is inconsistent. No (x,y) satisfies both.
∞ Infinite Solutions
CONDITION
m₁ = m₂, b₁ = b₂
Identical lines — every point on the line is a solution. System is dependent.
02 · Method 1 — Substitution
①

Substitution MethodBest when one variable is already isolated

1
Isolate one variable in either equation
Choose the equation with the simplest variable to isolate. Avoid fractions when possible.
2
Substitute expression into the other equation
Replace that variable in the second equation. Now you have one equation, one unknown.
3
Solve for the remaining variable
Simplify and solve the single-variable equation.
4
Back-substitute to find the other variable
Plug solved value into either original equation to find the second variable.
5
Check your solution in BOTH equations
Verify the (x, y) pair satisfies both original equations.
✏

Worked Example — SubstitutionStep by step with annotations

System:
y = 2x − 1   …(1)
3x + y = 9   …(2)
STEP 1
Eq (1) already has y isolated: y = 2x − 1
STEP 2
Sub into (2): 3x + (2x − 1) = 9
STEP 3
Simplify: 5x − 1 = 9 → 5x = 10 → x = 2
STEP 4
Back-sub: y = 2(2) − 1 = 3 → y = 3
CHECK
Eq (2): 3(2)+3 = 9 ✓
Solution: (2, 3)
03 · Method 2 — Elimination (Addition/Subtraction)
②

Elimination MethodBest when coefficients are easy to match

1
Align equations in standard form (Ax + By = C)
Both equations should have x and y on the left, constant on right.
2
Multiply to make coefficients of one variable equal (or opposite)
Multiply one or both equations by constants so that one variable will cancel out.
3
Add or subtract the equations to eliminate a variable
If signs are opposite → add. If signs are the same → subtract.
4
Solve for the remaining variable
The result is a one-variable equation — solve it directly.
5
Substitute back to find the other variable
Plug the found value into either original equation.
✓ WHEN TO USE ELIMINATION
Use elimination when coefficients of one variable are the same or simple multiples. Example: 2x + 3y = 7 and 4x − 3y = 5 → add directly (3y cancels)!
✏

Worked Example — EliminationMultiply to match, then cancel

System:
3x + 2y = 16  …(1)
5x − 2y = 8   …(2)
STEP 1
Already in standard form. 2y and −2y are already opposite!
STEP 2
Add (1) + (2): (3x+5x) + (2y−2y) = 16+8
STEP 3
8x = 24 → x = 3
STEP 4
Sub x=3 into (1): 9 + 2y = 16 → 2y = 7 → y = 7/2
CHECK
Eq (2): 15 − 7 = 8 ✓
Solution: (3, 7/2)
04 · Solving for Unknown Constants (k, a, b)
k

The 3 Constant RulesMost important SAT topic for this unit

✓ For ONE Solution
a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂
Coefficient ratios of x and y are NOT equal. Lines intersect. k must NOT satisfy the parallel condition.
✗ For NO Solution
a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂
x and y coefficient ratios are EQUAL, but constant ratio is DIFFERENT. Lines are parallel.
∞ For INFINITE Solutions
a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂
ALL three ratios are equal. Equations are multiples of each other — same line.
✓ QUICK METHOD
Rewrite both equations as y = mx + b first. Then compare slopes (m) and intercepts (b) directly — easier to see the condition.
✗

Find k for No SolutionSAT's most common unknown constant question

No Solution Condition
Same slope (m₁ = m₂), different y-intercept
Ratio of x-coefficients = ratio of y-coefficients ≠ ratio of constants
Example: Find k so that system has no solution
2x + 3y = 6   …(1)
4x + ky = 9   …(2)
STEP 1
Set x-coefficient ratio = y-coefficient ratio:
2/4 = 3/k
STEP 2
Cross multiply: 2k = 12 → k = 6
STEP 3
Verify constants: 6/9 = 2/3 ≠ 1/2 ✓ (constants differ → no solution)
k = 6 → No Solution
  • ▶Always verify that the constant ratio is not equal — otherwise it becomes infinite solutions
  • ▶If SAT gives you the system in slope-intercept form, just equate slopes and check intercepts
∞

Find k for Infinite SolutionsAll three ratios must be equal

Infinite Solutions Condition
One equation is a multiple of the other
All ratios equal: a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂
Example: Find k for infinite solutions
x + 2y = 4   …(1)
3x + ky = 12 …(2)
STEP 1
Find multiplier from x-coefficients: 3/1 = 3 → Eq (2) is 3 × Eq (1)
STEP 2
For y-coefficients: k = 3 × 2 = 6 → k = 6
STEP 3
Verify constant: 3 × 4 = 12 ✓ — all ratios equal → infinite solutions
k = 6 → Infinite Solutions
  • ▶Multiply the simpler equation by the constant ratio to match the other
  • ▶Check ALL three parts: x-coeff, y-coeff, and constant must all match the same multiplier
05 · Method Comparison & Graphing Method
③

Graphing MethodVisual — best for SAT graph questions

1
Rewrite each equation as y = mx + b
2
Plot both lines on the same coordinate plane
3
The intersection point = the solution (x, y)
⚖

All Three Methods — When to Use EachChoose the fastest method for each SAT question

Method Best Used When... Advantage Watch Out For Speed on SAT
Substitution One variable is already isolated (e.g., y = 3x + 1) or coefficient is 1 Clean and systematic; easy to check Fractions when dividing by non-1 coefficient ⚡⚡⚡ (fastest for isolated variable)
Elimination Coefficients are multiples of each other; both in standard form Avoids fractions; cancels variables cleanly Easy to make sign errors when subtracting ⚡⚡⚡ (fastest for matching coefficients)
Graphing SAT gives a graph or asks to identify intersection visually Instant visual check of solution type Imprecise for non-integer answers ⚡⚡ (good for verification)
Ratio / Proportion Finding value of unknown constant k for no/infinite solutions Direct and formula-based; no full solve needed Must check ALL three ratios for infinite solutions ⚡⚡⚡⚡ (fastest for SAT constant questions)
⚠ TRAP: "0 = 0" vs "0 = 5"
After elimination, if you get 0 = 0 → infinite solutions. If you get 0 = non-zero number (like 0 = 4) → no solution. These look identical to careless eyes!
⚠ TRAP: Substituting into the SAME equation
After solving for x, substitute into either original equation — NOT the manipulated one. Substituting into the equation you just used gives a trivial identity, not the answer.
06 · Word Problems & SAT Strategies
📝

Setting Up Word Problems as SystemsTranslate English → Math equations

Translation Guide
English PhraseMath Symbol / Setup
"sum of two numbers is 20"x + y = 20
"twice as many as"x = 2y
"total cost of x items at $3 and y items at $5 is $40"3x + 5y = 40
"difference between x and y is 6"x − y = 6
"combined age is 50; one is 4 years older"x + y = 50; x = y + 4
"rate × time = distance"r₁t = d₁; r₂t = d₂
Worked Word Problem
"A store sells apples for $1.50 and oranges for $2.00. Chloe buys 10 fruits and spends $17.00. How many of each did she buy?"

Let a = apples, o = oranges:
a + o = 10  (total fruits)
1.5a + 2o = 17  (total cost)

Solve: From eq 1: a = 10 − o
Sub: 1.5(10−o) + 2o = 17 → 15 − 1.5o + 2o = 17 → 0.5o = 2 → o = 4, a = 6
⚠️

SAT Traps & Power TipsThe difference between 700 and 800

⚠ TRAP 1: Missing the "infinite solutions" case
SAT often hides infinite solutions as: 2x + 4y = 6 and x + 2y = 3. Eq 2 is exactly half of Eq 1 — same line. Don't just check slopes!
⚠ TRAP 2: Forgetting to check the constant ratio
If slope ratios are equal but constant ratio is also equal → infinite solutions, NOT no solution. Always check the third ratio before declaring "no solution."
⚠ TRAP 3: Variable vs. coefficient confusion
In kx + 2y = 5, k is the coefficient of x — not the variable. Always identify what the unknown is (k) vs. what the variables are (x, y).
✓ TIP 1: Use elimination for most SAT systems
SAT systems are usually designed for elimination. Look for coefficients that sum to zero or are simple multiples — this is rarely an accident.
✓ TIP 2: The question often asks for x+y, not x and y separately
Don't solve the full system if SAT asks for x + y or 2x − y. Add/subtract the equations strategically to get exactly what's asked.
✓ TIP 3: Plug-in on multiple choice for unknowns
When finding constant k, try each answer choice — substitute back and check which value creates the required solution condition. Often faster than setting up ratios.
07 · Master Summary — All Rules at a Glance
Solution Types
  • ▶1 solution: different slopes → lines cross
  • ▶No solution: same slope, diff y-int → parallel
  • ▶Infinite: same slope & y-int → identical lines
  • ▶Test: eliminate → 0=0 or 0=k
Solving Methods
  • ▶Substitution: isolate → sub in → solve back
  • ▶Elimination: match coefficients → add/subtract
  • ▶Graphing: plot both lines → find intersection
  • ▶Always verify solution in BOTH equations
Unknown Constants (k)
  • ▶No soln: a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂
  • ▶Inf soln: a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂
  • ▶1 soln: a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂
  • ▶Fastest: convert to y=mx+b → compare
SAT Smart Moves
  • ▶Ask for x+y? Add equations directly
  • ▶0=0 → infinite; 0=5 → no solution
  • ▶Word problems: define variables first, then translate
  • ▶Try plugging in answer choices for k questions
