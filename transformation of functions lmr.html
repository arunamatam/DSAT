02 · Vertical Shifts — f(x) ± k
↑↓

Shifting Up: f(x) + kEvery point moves up by k units

Rule
y = f(x) + k   (k > 0)
All y-coordinates increase by k. x-coordinates unchanged. y-intercept increases by k.
  • ▶Vertex, intercepts, and all points shift up by exactly k
  • ▶Shape & orientation of graph do NOT change
  • ▶Example: f(x)=x² → f(x)+4 = x²+4 — vertex moves from (0,0) to (0,4)
↕

Shifting Down: f(x) − kEvery point moves down by k units

Rule
y = f(x) − k   (k > 0)
All y-coordinates decrease by k. x-intercepts change (zeros shift). y-intercept decreases by k.
  • ▶When shifted down enough, new x-intercepts may appear (or disappear)
  • ▶Example: x² has no x-intercepts at vertex (0,4), but x²−4 crosses x-axis at x=±2
  • ▶SAT: "Find value of c so that f(x)+c has exactly one zero" → vertex touches x-axis
📝

Vertical Shift SAT QuestionsMost common ways SAT tests this

Q-Type A: Identify shift from equation
f(x) = x² → g(x) = x² + 5
→ g is f shifted UP 5
Q-Type B: Find k from a point
g(x) = f(x) + k. If f(2)=3 and
g(2)=7, then k = 7−3 = 4
Q-Type C: Interpret y-intercept change
f(x) has y-int = 3.
f(x)+6 has y-int = 9
(y-int shifts by same k)
✓ KEY INSIGHT
Vertical shifts ONLY change y-values. The x-coordinates of all points are unchanged — only their heights change.
03 · Horizontal Shifts — f(x ± h) · The Counterintuitive Rule
→

Shift Right: f(x − h)Minus inside → moves RIGHT — counterintuitive!

Rule (h > 0)
y = f(x − h)
Replace x with (x−h) inside. Graph moves RIGHT by h units. All x-values + h.
  • ▶Why counterintuitive: f(x−3)=0 when x−3=0, i.e. x=3 (shifted right to 3)
  • ▶Vertex of f(x−h)² is at x=h (positive h = moved right)
  • ▶Example: (x−2)² → parabola with vertex at (2, 0) — shifted right 2
←

Shift Left: f(x + h)Plus inside → moves LEFT — counterintuitive!

Rule (h > 0)
y = f(x + h)
Replace x with (x+h) inside. Graph moves LEFT by h units. All x-values − h.
  • ▶Example: (x+3)² → parabola with vertex at (−3, 0) — shifted LEFT 3
  • ▶To find vertex x: set the expression inside = 0 and solve
  • ▶SAT: "If g(x)=f(x+4), which point on g corresponds to (2,5) on f?" → (−2, 5)
💡

Decoding Horizontal ShiftsInside the parentheses — always opposite!

TRICK: "What makes inside = 0?"
f(x − 3): set x−3=0 → x=3 → shifts RIGHT 3
f(x + 5): set x+5=0 → x=−5 → shifts LEFT 5
f(2x − 4): set 2x−4=0 → x=2 → shifts RIGHT 2
Key Points That Move With the Graph
Original f(x) Shift Right 3: f(x−3) Shift Left 2: f(x+2)
(0, 5)(3, 5)(−2, 5)
(2, 0)(5, 0)(0, 0)
(−1, 3)(2, 3)(−3, 3)
⚠ #1 SAT TRAP: SIGN OF HORIZONTAL SHIFT
g(x) = f(x−5) → shifted RIGHT 5, NOT left 5. The minus sign inside always means right. This is the single most common transformation mistake.
04 · Reflections — Flipping Over Axes
⊥x

Reflect Over x-axis: −f(x)All y-values flip sign — graph turns upside down

Rule
y = −f(x)
Negate the output. Every point (x, y) → (x, −y). Graph is mirror image across x-axis.
  • ▶Parabola opening up → opens down (and vice versa)
  • ▶x-intercepts (zeros) stay the same — they're on the x-axis so y=0 → −0=0
  • ▶y-intercept changes sign: if f(0)=4, then −f(0)=−4
  • ▶Example: f(x)=x² → −f(x)=−x² (opens down)
  • ▶SAT: −f(x) increases when f(x) decreases
⊥y

Reflect Over y-axis: f(−x)All x-values flip sign — graph mirrors left-right

Rule
y = f(−x)
Negate the input. Every point (x, y) → (−x, y). Left and right sides swap.
  • ▶y-intercept stays the same: f(0) = f(−0) = f(0)
  • ▶x-intercepts mirror: if f(3)=0, then f(−3)=0 in f(−x)
  • ▶Even functions are unchanged: f(−x)=f(x) for even f
  • ▶Example: f(x)=2ˣ → f(−x)=2⁻ˣ (decaying instead of growing)
  • ▶SAT: Tests whether a graph is the y-axis reflection
05 · Vertical & Horizontal Stretches and Compressions
↕+

Vertical Stretcha · f(x), |a| > 1

Rule — outside multiplier
a · f(x), |a| > 1
All y-values multiplied by a. Graph gets taller/narrower.
  • ▶x-intercepts unchanged (y=0 → a·0=0)
  • ▶Vertex/peak moves further from x-axis
  • ▶Example: 3x² is taller/narrower than x²
↕−

Vertical Compressa · f(x), 0 < |a| < 1

Rule — outside fraction
a · f(x), 0<|a|<1
All y-values × a (fraction). Graph gets flatter/wider.
  • ▶Graph moves closer to x-axis
  • ▶Example: ½x² is flatter/wider than x²
  • ▶SAT: "Stretch or compress?" → compare a to 1
↔−

Horizontal Compressf(bx), |b| > 1

Rule — inside multiplier
f(bx), |b| > 1
x-values divided by b. Graph gets narrower (horizontally squeezed).
  • ▶b=2 means graph is half as wide
  • ▶Counterintuitive: b>1 COMPRESSES (not stretches)
  • ▶Example: sin(2x) completes cycle in π instead of 2π
↔+

Horizontal Stretchf(bx), 0 < |b| < 1

Rule — inside small multiplier
f(bx), 0<|b|<1
x-values divided by b (b<1 → dividing by a fraction = multiplying). Graph gets wider.
  • ▶b=½ means graph is twice as wide
  • ▶Horizontal scale factor = 1/b
  • ▶Example: sin(½x) has period 4π (doubled)
06 · Combined Transformations & Vertex Form
⊕

Combined: a · f(b(x−h)) + kRead off all four transformations at once

General Transform Form
g(x) = a · f(b(x − h)) + k
a = vert. scale & reflect  |  b = horiz. scale & reflect  |  h = horiz. shift  |  k = vert. shift
Decode: g(x) = −3 · f(2(x − 4)) + 7
a=−3
Vertical stretch × 3, then flip over x-axis (negative)
b=2
Horizontal compression by factor of 2 (narrower)
h=4
Horizontal shift: RIGHT 4 (minus inside = right)
k=7
Vertical shift: UP 7
✓ APPLY TRANSFORMATIONS IN ORDER
①Horiz. shift (h) → ②Horiz. scale (b) → ③Reflect → ④Vert. scale (a) → ⑤Vert. shift (k). Doing them out of order gives wrong graphs!

SAT Traps & Key InsightsThe patterns that decide your score

⚠ TRAP 1: Inside sign = opposite direction
f(x−5)→ RIGHT 5 not left. f(x+5) → LEFT 5. Always set inside=0 to find where graph moved.
⚠ TRAP 2: b inside compresses (not stretches)
f(2x) with b=2 makes the graph NARROWER (compressed by ½), not wider. "Big b = narrow" is the rule.
⚠ TRAP 3: f(x+h)+k: both shifts active
f(x+3)−2 means LEFT 3 AND DOWN 2 simultaneously. Don't forget to apply both!
✓ TIP: Use a known point to verify
If f(2)=5, then for g(x)=f(x−3)+1: g(5)=f(5−3)+1=f(2)+1=6. Tracing transformed points verifies your equation.
✓ TIP: Vertex form tells all
For SAT quadratic questions, always convert to vertex form. It reveals the vertex, axis of symmetry, direction, and all transformations at once.
✓ SAT SPEED SHORTCUT
For "which graph shows f(x−2)+3?": move every key point (vertex, intercepts) RIGHT 2, UP 3. Match to answer choices by checking those specific coordinates.
