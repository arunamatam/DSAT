01 · Understanding Slope
📐

What is Slope?The steepness & direction of a line

The Slope Formula
m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁)
Also written as: m = Δy / Δx = rise / run
Given two points (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂) on a line
  • ▶Rise = vertical change (Δy) — how far up or down
  • ▶Run = horizontal change (Δx) — how far left or right
  • ▶Order of points doesn't matter as long as you're consistent with (x₁,y₁) and (x₂,y₂)
  • ▶Slope is the same between any two points on the same line
📊

4 Types of SlopeKnow them at a glance for SAT

↗ Positive Slope
Goes UP left→right
Line rises as x increases. Example: m = 2 or m = ½
↘ Negative Slope
Goes DOWN left→right
Line falls as x increases. Example: m = −3 or m = −½
→ Zero Slope
Horizontal line
Equation: y = k (constant). Rise = 0, so m = 0/run = 0
↕ Undefined Slope
Vertical line
Equation: x = k. Run = 0 → division by zero → undefined
⚠ SAT TRAP: Zero vs. Undefined
"Slope of zero" (horizontal) and "undefined slope" (vertical) are completely different. A horizontal line has equation y = 3; a vertical line has equation x = 3. Confusing these is a top SAT error.
02 · Three Forms of Linear Equations
①

Slope-Intercept FormMost common on SAT

Formula
y = mx + b
m = slope  |  b = y-intercept (where line crosses y-axis)
  • ▶Read slope directly: coefficient of x is m
  • ▶Read y-intercept directly: constant term is b
  • ▶Example: y = 3x − 5 → m = 3, b = −5
  • ▶SAT tip: Most "find the equation" answers are in this form
②

Point-Slope FormWhen you have a point + slope

Formula
y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)
Use when given: a point (x₁, y₁) and a slope m
📌 Step-by-Step Example
Given: point (2, 5), slope m = 3
Step 1
Plug in: y − 5 = 3(x − 2)
Step 2
Distribute: y − 5 = 3x − 6
Step 3
Solve: y = 3x − 1
  • ▶Best when SAT gives you a graph and asks for the equation
  • ▶Note the subtraction sign — it's y minus y₁, not plus
  • ▶Works with any point on the line, not just the y-intercept
  • ▶Convert to slope-intercept form to match SAT answer choices
③

Standard FormCommon in SAT word problems

Formula
Ax + By = C
A, B, C are integers; A > 0; no fractions. Slope = −A/B; y-int = C/B
🔄 Converting to Slope-Intercept
3x + 2y = 8
→ 2y = −3x + 8   (subtract 3x)
→ y = −(3/2)x + 4   (divide by 2)
∴ m = −3/2, b = 4
  • ▶To find x-intercept: set y = 0 → x = C/A
  • ▶To find y-intercept: set x = 0 → y = C/B
  • ▶Slope = −A/B (memorize for quick SAT answers)
  • ▶SAT often gives standard form; you must convert to slope-intercept
03 · Graphing a Line & Finding Intercepts
📈

How to Graph a LineStep-by-step from equation y = mx + b

1
Identify m and b
From y = 2x + 3: m = 2, b = 3. The y-intercept is the point (0, 3).
2
Plot the y-intercept
Place a point at (0, b) on the y-axis. This is your starting point.
3
Use slope to find next point
m = 2 = 2/1 → go right 1, up 2. Plot that point: (1, 5).
4
Draw the line
Connect the two (or more) points with a straight line extending in both directions.
🎯

X & Y InterceptsWhere the line crosses each axis

Y-Intercept
Set x = 0
The point where the line crosses the y-axis. Always has form (0, b).
X-Intercept
Set y = 0
The point where the line crosses the x-axis. Always has form (a, 0).
Worked Example: 3x + 2y = 12
Y-Intercept (x=0)
3(0) + 2y = 12
2y = 12
y = 6 → (0, 6)
X-Intercept (y=0)
3x + 2(0) = 12
3x = 12
x = 4 → (4, 0)
✓ SAT SHORTCUT
For Ax + By = C: x-intercept = C/A, y-intercept = C/B. No algebra needed — just divide!
04 · Parallel, Perpendicular & Special Lines
∥

Parallel LinesSame slope, different y-intercept

Condition
m₁ = m₂
Two lines are parallel if and only if their slopes are equal (and they are distinct lines)
  • ▶Lines y = 2x + 5 and y = 2x − 3 are parallel (both m = 2)
  • ▶Parallel lines never intersect — no solution to the system
  • ▶SAT: "Which equation is parallel to…?" → keep m, change b
⊥

Perpendicular LinesNegative reciprocal slopes

Condition
m₁ × m₂ = −1
Equivalently: m₂ = −1/m₁ (flip fraction, change sign)
HOW TO FIND NEGATIVE RECIPROCAL
m = 3 → m⊥ = −1/3
m = −2/5 → m⊥ = 5/2
m = 1 → m⊥ = −1
  • ▶Perpendicular lines meet at a right angle (90°)
  • ▶Product of slopes = −1 always (except horizontal/vertical pair)
★

Special LinesHorizontal, vertical & axes

Horizontal Line
y = k
Slope = 0. Passes through (0, k). Parallel to x-axis.
Vertical Line
x = k
Slope = undefined. Passes through (k, 0). Parallel to y-axis.
PropertyHorizontal y=kVertical x=k
Slopem = 0undefined
y-intercept(0, k)None (unless k=0)
x-interceptNone (unless k=0)(k, 0)
Axis direction→ horizontal↕ vertical
05 · Converting Between Forms & Distance/Midpoint
⇄

Converting Between FormsMaster this for SAT speed

CONVERSION FLOW
Slope-Intercept
y = mx + b
⇄
Point-Slope
y−y₁=m(x−x₁)
⇄
Standard Form
Ax + By = C
From → ToWhat to doExample
Slope-int → Standard Move mx to left; multiply to clear fractions y=2x+3 → 2x−y=−3
Standard → Slope-int Solve for y; isolate y on left 3x+2y=6 → y=−(3/2)x+3
Point-slope → Slope-int Distribute m, move constant to right y−1=2(x−3) → y=2x−5
Two points → Equation Find m first, then use point-slope form (1,2)&(3,8): m=3 → y=3x−1
✓ SPEED TRICK FOR SAT
When given two points, skip point-slope — compute m, then plug into y = mx + b using either point to find b. Faster and fewer steps!
📏

Distance & MidpointBetween two points on a coordinate plane

Distance Formula
d = √((x₂−x₁)²+(y₂−y₁)²)
Pythagorean theorem in disguise: a²+b²=c², where a=Δx, b=Δy, c=distance
Midpoint Formula
M = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2)
Average the x-coordinates and average the y-coordinates separately
Worked Example: A(1, 2) and B(5, 6)
Distance:
d = √((5−1)² + (6−2)²)
d = √(16 + 16)
d = √32 = 4√2
Midpoint:
M = ((1+5)/2, (2+6)/2)
M = (6/2, 8/2)
M = (3, 4)
06 · SAT-Specific Strategies & Common Question Types
📝

Common SAT Question TypesLines & Slopes appear in ~20% of Math questions

Question TypeWhat to doKey Formula
Find equation from graph Read y-intercept from graph; pick 2 points to get slope y = mx + b
Find equation from 2 points Compute m = Δy/Δx; plug a point in to find b m = (y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁)
Parallel line equation Keep same slope m; substitute the new given point to find b Same m, new b
Perpendicular line equation Flip slope & change sign; substitute point to find b m⊥ = −1/m
Systems of 2 linear equations Set equations equal or use substitution/elimination Intersection point
No solution / infinite solutions Same slope = parallel (no solution) OR identical (infinite) Compare slopes
Interpret slope in context Slope = rate of change; units are y-units per x-unit m = Δy/Δx
Interpret y-intercept in context b = starting value when x = 0 b at x = 0
✓ CONTEXT INTERPRETATION
If a word problem says "initial value" → that's the y-intercept (b).
If it says "rate of change / per unit / each" → that's the slope (m).
⚠️

SAT Traps & Power TipsAvoid these mistakes for a perfect score

⚠ TRAP 1: Sign of slope from standard form
In 2x + 3y = 6, slope is NOT 2/3. It is −2/3. Always convert to y = mx + b first, or use formula m = −A/B.
⚠ TRAP 2: Confusing "no slope" with "slope of 0"
"No slope" = undefined (vertical line). "Slope of zero" = horizontal line. These are opposites — don't mix them up.
⚠ TRAP 3: Perpendicular slope with negative original
If m = −3/4, then m⊥ = +4/3 (flip AND change sign → double negative = positive). Many students forget the sign flip.
⚠ TRAP 4: Assuming lines are parallel without checking
Check slopes! Lines with different slopes always intersect at exactly one point. Equal slopes with same b = same line (infinite solutions).
✓ TIP: Back-substitution shortcut
On multiple choice, plug the given point into each answer choice — the one that works AND has the correct slope is your answer. Often faster than solving from scratch.
✓ TIP: Sketching saves you
Always sketch a quick graph for lines problems. A 5-second sketch eliminates wrong answer traps — especially for sign-of-slope and intercept questions.
